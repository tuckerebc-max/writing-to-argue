# Provenance, rights, and currentness

Treat every source detail as a traceable record rather than an undifferentiated citation.

## Minimum source record

Record a stable `source_id`, author or organization, title, date/version, locator system, authority role, direct-reading status, access or license status, and the source's limitations relative to the claim.

## Minimum evidence record

Record the source ID, exact locator, whether the content is quoted, paraphrased, observed, or inferred, verification status, relevance to the claim, and known limitations. Never invent a quote, locator, source claim, current policy, legal rule, stakeholder position, or research finding.

## Rights boundary

Use protected material as a source lead or for user-supplied analysis only when permitted. Prefer short, attributed paraphrase and links. Do not package protected textbook, AP, rubric, student, dataset, or assessment content as reusable assets without documented permission.

## Currentness boundary

Current course, policy, legal, civic, institutional, or program claims require a dated official or otherwise authoritative source and a currentness check. If currentness is unresolved, return `CURRENTNESS_REVIEW` or `NEEDS_SOURCE` rather than silently using an old record.
